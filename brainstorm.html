Brainstorm · v0.7 · web app first

Irembo Companion

An AI guide that turns Rwanda's e-gov platform from a stack of isolated forms into one navigable life-event journey. v0.7 re-sequencing: build the standalone Journey Explorer web app first — anyone (citizen or agent) answers a few questions and sees their whole personalized path, including the steps people miss. The agent side-panel overlay comes later, once the journey model has proven itself.

Subject: irembo.gov.rw Wedge: public web app · beside (not integrated) · human-in-the-loop Model: free public good (grant-funded) · Kinyarwanda-first

1The core mismatch

Irembo is built around transactions (one service = one form = one payment). But citizens don't have "transactions" — they have life events: getting married, buying land, opening a business, sending a child abroad. A single life event silently fans out into 3–7 services across 4–5 agencies, each with its own prerequisites. The platform never shows the citizen this shape. The agent holds it — in their head.

Pain

Invisible prerequisites

You discover you needed a national ID, a tax clearance, or the other party's birth certificate only after you've started — and paid.

Pain

No sense of "when"

Is this 10 minutes or 3 weeks? Does step 2 block step 5? The citizen can't plan around a process they can't see.

Pain

Fragmented status

Five applications across five agencies = five places to check, no single answer to "where am I overall?"

The agent isn't selling a transaction. They're selling certainty and sequencing — knowing which door to knock on, in what order, with what in hand. That knowledge is exactly what software can encode.

DDDeep dive — "Open a business," end to end

The worked example behind §3. Running case: a citizen opening a small restaurant as a sole proprietorship ("enterprise"). The crucial finding for the product: the RDB certificate — what almost everyone means by "registering a business" — is only step 3 of 8. The journey has a long, invisible compliance tail, and rejections cluster at the boundaries between agencies, which is precisely where agents earn their fee.

0

Decide the legal structure

Sole proprietorship / "enterprise" (simplest, one owner) vs. company / Ltd (shareholders, articles of association, can raise capital). This choice silently changes every later step.

Decision · not on IremboNo feeMinutes
Where it goes wrong
  • Picks "company" when an enterprise was intended → asked for articles & directors they don't have, and abandons.
  • Picks "enterprise" for a multi-partner venture → has to re-register later.
1

Valid National ID (the root of trust)

Every applicant is keyed on their NIDA identity. Foreigners need a passport + valid residence permit instead.

NIDAFreePrerequisite
Common rejections
  • ID expired, or name spelling on the form ≠ NIDA record → identity mismatch.
  • Applicant under 18 — minors cannot register a business.
  • Foreigner without a valid residence permit / visa class that allows trading.
2

Business-name search & reservation

Check the name is available and permissible before committing to it.

RDBFreeMinutes
Common rejections
  • Name identical or confusingly similar to an existing registered business.
  • Contains restricted words ("Bank", "Insurance", "National", "Rwanda", "University"…) without authorization.
  • Implies a regulated activity the applicant isn't licensed for.
3

Business registration → Certificate of Registration

The actual RDB registration. Capture activity, address, owner/shareholders, capital. Certificate is typically issued same-day to ~2 days — and registration is free. This is the step everyone thinks is the whole job.

RDBFreeSame-day–2 days
Common rejections
  • Wrong / mismatched ISIC activity code vs. the business actually described.
  • Address hierarchy (Village → Cell → Sector → District) doesn't resolve.
  • Company path only: shareholding doesn't total 100%, missing directors, missing articles of association.
  • Poor document quality — blurry ID scan, wrong file format.
4

TIN issued automatically (+ VAT if over threshold)

RDB and RRA are integrated, so a Tax Identification Number is issued with registration — no separate step. But tax obligations start now. VAT registration becomes mandatory above ~RWF 20M annual turnover.

RRA (auto)FreeAutomatic
Where it goes wrong (silent, not a "rejection")
  • Owner doesn't realise they must now file declarations — even nil returns — and accrues penalties.
  • Crosses the VAT threshold without registering / acquiring an EBM e-invoicing device.
5

Trading license (district level) — the first hidden step

The RDB certificate does not grant permission to operate. A separate annual trading license / tax is paid to the District, on a different channel from the RDB flow.

District / SectorAnnual paid taxDays
Common rejections
  • No proof of premises (lease agreement / land title) for the trading address.
  • Location not zoned for the activity (e.g. a bar too close to a school/church).
  • Arrears on a prior year's trading license block renewal.
6

Sector permit(s) — the big one agents catch

For a restaurant: a health/hygiene inspection certificate (and a liquor permit if serving alcohol). Other activities route to other regulators — pharmacy → Rwanda FDA + Pharmacy Council, school → REB/MINEDUC, transport → RURA, tourism/hotel → RDB grading. This is the step most people don't know exists until an inspector arrives.

Activity-specific regulatorVariesDays–weeks
Common rejections
  • Starts operating on the strength of the RDB certificate alone — operating illegally until inspected/fined.
  • Applies to the wrong regulator, or fails the premises inspection.
  • Doesn't know the permit was required at all → the single most valuable thing an agent knows.
7

RSSB + bank account + EBM (operational setup)

Register with RSSB once hiring (social security for employees). Open a business bank account (needs certificate + TIN). Acquire an EBM e-invoicing device if VAT-registered.

RSSB · Bank · RRAMixedOngoing
Where it goes wrong
  • Hires staff without registering them with RSSB → liability on inspection.
  • Bank rejects account opening because registered details ≠ ID details (loops back to step 1).

Rejection failure-points → what the Companion does

Every rejection above is a feature spec. This is the seed of the dependency-rule dataset (Q4) and the side-panel's warning logic.

The headline insight: the RDB certificate is necessary but not sufficient. Steps 5–7 (the violet "hidden tail") are where citizens get stuck, operate illegally, or come back to an agent confused. Irembo/RDB treat step 3 as "done"; the agent's whole value is knowing the tail. Encode the tail, and you've bottled the agent's expertise.

{ }The data model — this deep dive, encoded

The journey above is now real data, not prose. The design is artifact-based: services consume and produce artifacts (national-id, certificate, trading-license…), so the dependency graph, path-finding, cost/time roll-ups, and "what's blocking me" are all derived by matching requires ↔ produces. Rules live on each service and carry the mitigation, so the side-panel's warning logic falls straight out of the data.

data/schema.json — the model (JSON Schema) data/dataset.json — shared catalog + journeys (10 agencies · 21 artifacts · 20 services · 27 rules · 2 journeys)

Core entities: agencies · artifacts (graph edges) · services (nodes, with requires/produces/cost/duration/appliesWhen/rules) · journeys (ordered life events, each step flagged hidden for the invisible tail). A sample node:

{
  "id": "svc.sector-permit-restaurant",
  "agency": "agency.health-regulator",
  "appliesWhen": { "var": "activity", "op": "in",
                   "value": ["restaurant","food","bar","hotel"] },
  "requires": ["art.certificate-of-registration", "art.premises-proof"],
  "produces": ["art.health-hygiene-certificate"],
  "rules": [{
    "severity": "high",
    "failureMode": "illegal-operation",
    "message": "Operates on the RDB certificate alone — illegal until inspected.",
    "mitigation": "Persistent 'you still need a sector permit' banner."
  }]
}

Because it's data, a tiny engine computes the Phase 0/1 views for any citizen profile. Running it for a below-VAT-threshold restaurant that will hire:

Referential integrity + graph completeness: PASS

=== Open a restaurant — path for (restaurant, hiring, below VAT) ===
   #0 Decide legal structure            (free,  5-30 min)
   #1 Verify National ID                (free,  instant)
   #2 Secure proof of premises          (var,   0-30 days)
   #3 Business-name reservation         (free,  5-60 min)
   #4 Business registration (RDB)       (free,  0-2 days)
   [HIDDEN] #6 Trading license          (var,   1-7 days)
   [HIDDEN] #7 Health/hygiene permit    (var,   1-4 weeks)
   [HIDDEN] #8 RSSB employer reg.        (free,  1-3 days)
   #9 Open business bank account        (var,   1-5 days)

Roll-up: 9 steps | 3 hidden-tail | ~10-75 days | 5 high-severity rules
Steps pruned for this profile (VAT + EBM, below threshold): 2
What this proves: conditional pruning, hidden-tail detection, cost/time roll-up and rule activation are all derived from the data — zero hard-coded logic. The dataset is tagged "sourceConfidence": "needs-verification" — every fee/threshold marked CONFIRM is what the agent-interview script (Q4) nails down.

2×Second journey — land transfer (the engine generalizes)

Adding land transfer required no new engine code — just a second journey instance in the same catalog. It reuses svc.verify-national-id and art.national-id across both journeys (the foundation of a future document vault), and adds RLMUA + Notary as new agencies. Crucially it reveals a different shape of the same problem.

Pattern A · business

Hidden tail comes after

You get the headline RDB certificate early, then discover the trading license + sector permits you still owe. The trap is thinking you're done.

Pattern B · land

Hidden work comes before

Spousal consent, encumbrance/mortgage checks, tax clearance — all must happen before the "apply on Irembo" step everyone thinks is the transfer. The trap is thinking you can start.

Same underlying truth in both: the visible Irembo transaction is a small slice of the real journey. The companion's value is rendering the invisible part — whichever end it sits on.

The engine prices the path per citizen profile. Same journey, two sellers:

=== Transfer land — married seller, no mortgage, sole-owned ===
   Verify National ID            (free)        <- SHARED with business journey
   Verify land title & ownership (free)
   [HIDDEN] Encumbrance check     (free)
   [HIDDEN] Obtain spousal consent (var)       <- THE classic rejection
   [HIDDEN] Land-tax clearance    (var)
   Notarize sale agreement       (var)
   Pay transfer fee              (var)
   Apply for land transfer (RLMUA)(free)
   Roll-up: 8/10 steps | 3 hidden | ~6-54 days | 6 high-severity rules

=== Same journey — married, mortgaged, FAMILY land (worst case) ===
   ...+ [HIDDEN] Mortgage release  (1-4 weeks)
   ...+ [HIDDEN] Co-owner consent   (1-14 days)
   Roll-up: 10/10 steps | 5 hidden | ~14-96 days | 8 high-severity rules
Why this matters for the wedge: two profiles for the same goal differ by 4× in time and two whole steps. A citizen can't price that. An agent prices it from memory — unevenly. The engine prices it identically every time, which is exactly the trust the side-panel sells.

▣The side-panel extension — Phase 2 (after the web app)

Re-sequenced to follow the Journey Explorer web app (§7). Once the journey model is proven publicly, this overlay brings it into the agent's workflow: a browser side-panel next to Irembo/RDB/RLMUA that reads the page they're on, figures out which step of which journey they're in, and overlays the map + warnings + prefill — driven by the same data. The fragile DOM coupling is pushed out of code into a config file, so a portal change is a data edit, not a release. (Spec already drafted — see below.)

Rules fire at three moments (by failureMode)

On-intake
eligibility rules — ID expired, applicant a minor, missing spousal consent. Red banner that persists until resolved.
On-field
completeness / quality rules — name taken, wrong ISIC code. Inline chip + suggestion as the agent types.
Pre-submit
rejection rules — fired on focus of the real submit button: "2 likely-rejection issues, review first." Advisory, never a hard block.

silent-liability & illegal-operation rules don't fire at a moment — they raise a persistent reminder ("you still need a sector permit") that survives across sessions for that client.

The "beside" boundary (hard line): the extension reads the DOM, computes, suggests, and can fill fields — but it never clicks submit. Pre-flight checks are advisory warnings before the agent's own click. Needs zero RISA cooperation and keeps the human accountable (Q3). The only government-coupled code is adapters.json; a self-test flags dead selectors so we learn the portal changed before agents do.
And the loop closes: when an agent dismisses a warning, submits, and a step is later rejected, the panel asks them to tag the reason. That stream corrects the CONFIRM fees and flips sourceConfidence from needs-verification to verified — the agents become the sensor network that keeps the rule base honest (Q4).

5The AI Companion — what it actually is

Not a chatbot bolted onto a portal. A journey orchestration layer with a conversational + visual front end.

Feature

Goal → Path

Citizen states a life goal ("I want to open a salon", "marry in August"). Companion returns the full ordered path with prerequisites, costs, durations, and which agency each touches.

Feature

Visual journey map

The graph from §3, personalized: green = done, blue = ready now, grey = blocked-until. The citizen sees "what to expect when" at a glance.

Feature

Pre-flight eligibility

Before any payment: "Your ID expires in 12 days — renew first or this will be rejected." Catches failure before money and time are spent.

Feature

Unified status tracker

One timeline across all agencies. Push/SMS when a step unblocks the next. Answers "where am I overall?"

Feature

Document co-pilot

Camera capture + on-device quality/format check + auto-fill of repeated fields (name, ID, address) across the whole chain.

Feature

Multilingual & voice

Kinyarwanda-first, voice in/out, USSD/IVR fallback for non-smartphone citizens. Meets people where literacy and devices are.

Provocation (v0.2): In an agent-first world the killer surface isn't a citizen app — it's a side-panel beside Irembo showing the agent "this client also needs X, and their ID expires in 12 days." Same journey engine, but the first user is the human already doing the work. Zero RISA integration required.

6Backend & engine gaps (why this is hard)

The visible UX problems are symptoms. Underneath, the architecture is likely transaction-centric with weak cross-agency orchestration. Honest list of what's probably missing.

Gap

No service-dependency model

Prerequisites live in policy docs and agents' heads, not as queryable data. Nothing to path-find over.

Gap

No orchestration / saga engine

Each service is fire-and-forget. Nothing coordinates a multi-step, multi-agency workflow or handles partial failure/rollback.

Gap

Fragmented identity & status

Status lives per-agency (NIDA, RDB, RRA, RLMUA, RNP). No unified citizen timeline / event bus.

Gap

No eligibility/rules engine

Rules (age, validity, prior-debt, residency) aren't centralized, so pre-validation isn't possible and rejections happen late.

Gap

Document reuse

The same birth cert / ID is re-uploaded per service. No citizen document vault with consented reuse.

Gap

Integration surface

Companion needs read APIs (status, eligibility, catalog) the platform may not expose. Likely needs an API gateway / event layer.

Architecture bet: introduce a Journey Service — a graph of services + a rules engine + an event subscription to each agency. The companion is just the friendliest client of that service. The same engine could power the agent console and the official portal too.

8Decisions locked & tensions to manage

v0.2 settles the six open questions. The decisions are coherent, but two pairs pull against each other — naming them keeps us honest.

Locked

Beside, not with

Independent companion — overlay / deep-link, no RISA integration in v1. Faster to ship, no permission gate.

Locked

Agent tool first

Few, tech-comfortable, monetized, feel rejection pain. The beachhead before direct-to-citizen.

Locked

Human-in-the-loop

AI proposes the path & pre-fills; the agent reviews and submits. Trust is earned before any autonomy.

Locked

Real dependency rules

We source authoritative rules (not reverse-engineer guesses) — via research + institutional relationships.

Locked

Kinyarwanda + USSD/IVR essential

Non-negotiable access. In v1 the agent is the human bridge; v3 brings voice/USSD direct to citizens.

Locked

Free public good

Free to citizens. Realistically grant/donor-funded until proven enough for government adoption.

Tension 1 — "beside" vs. "real rules" + "gov-funded": if we're not official, why would gov give us authoritative rules or money? Resolution: technically beside, institutionally with — cultivate RISA/agency relationships for the rule data and an eventual adoption path (Phase 4), while staying independent in the stack.
Tension 2 — "agent tool" vs. "free public good": agents are the one segment that would happily pay. Resolution: keep it free to citizens; the agent tool is the funded distribution & proving ground (grant-backed), not a revenue play.
